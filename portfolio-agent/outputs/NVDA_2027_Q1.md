## Earnings Brief — NVDA Q1 FY2027 (May 20, 2026)

### 1. One-line verdict
Massive beat — $82B revenue (+85% YoY, +20% QoQ) shattered records across revenue, operating income, and free cash flow, with Blackwell demand exceeding supply across every customer tier and Jensen declaring VeraRubin will be "more successful than even Grace Blackwell."

### 2. Key numbers
- Revenue: $82B, +85% YoY, +20% QoQ — record; $13.5B sequential gain also a record
- Data center: $75B, +92% YoY, +21% QoQ; Hyperscale $38B (+12% QoQ), ACIE $37B (+31% QoQ)
- Edge computing: $6.4B, +29% YoY; consumer modestly soft on memory/system prices
- Non-GAAP gross margin: 75%, flat sequentially — held well through Blackwell ramp
- Free cash flow: $49B — record, up from $35B in Q4
- Capital return: Dividend raised $0.01 → $0.25/quarter; $80B new buyback + $39B remaining; ~50% FCF return plan
- Q2 guidance: $91B ±2%; non-GAAP GM ~75% ±50bps
- VeraRubin: production Q3 2026; 35x inference throughput vs. Blackwell; $1T Blackwell+Rubin revenue forecast 2025–2027

### 3. Management tone
Overall confidence: EXTREMELY HIGH — Jensen expansive throughout; no hedging on demand or competitive position.
Key phrases:
"Giving us full confidence in $1 trillion in Blackwell and Rubin revenue we foresee from 2025 through calendar 2027."
"Every single frontier model company will jump on VeraRubin from the get go."
New segment reporting (Hyperscale / ACIE / Edge) introduced — signals business complexity has grown; bullish on ACIE as underappreciated TAM.

### 4. What analysts asked about
1. New segment rationale (Moore/Morgan Stanley): Jensen framed ACIE (AI clouds, industrial, sovereign) as a "second category" growing 31% QoQ that semi-custom chips don't serve — NVDA's TAM well beyond hyperscaler CapEx narrative.
2. NVDA growth vs. hyperscaler CapEx (Reitzes/Melius): Jensen asserted NVDA should grow faster because ACIE + physical AI are additive. No deflection.
3. VeraRubin inference share (Muse/Cantor): Anthropic partnership (previously near-zero) cited as major recent share gain; "growing share in inference very, very quickly."

### 5. Red flags (if any)
- China: zero revenue; H200 imports uncertain despite licenses; deliberately excluded from Q2 guidance.
- Consumer edge softness: "fell modestly due to higher memory and system prices" — monitor PC/GPU consumer trajectory.
- OpEx growing "upper forties" YoY for FY27 — R&D + AI tools accelerating; margin leverage depends on revenue outpacing.
- LPX inference chips called "niche" by Jensen — bears watching if custom silicon competition broadens.

### 6. Watch list for next quarter
1. Q2 actual vs. $91B guide — NVDA has beaten guidance by 5–15% for six consecutive quarters; VeraRubin transition makes this the key execution test.
2. VeraRubin Q3 production ramp confirmation — Jensen said "starting Q3"; watch for on-time commencement and initial hyperscale pulls.
3. China re-entry — any approved H200 imports or new export framework = unmodeled revenue upside.
